## Composition in performance

The Homer Multitext project situates the epics in oral tradition; the [source record here](../external-sources/chs-homer-multitext.md) is metadata only, so this is a pointer to that body of work rather than a claim carried by a preserved text. Formulaic language is therefore more than ornament: recurrent phrases and scene-patterns belong to the compositional system.

## What repeats—and changes

The preserved complete text contains recurring dawns, assemblies, voyages, sacrifices, feasts, baths, laments, speeches, and arrivals.[^butler] Epithets identify figures through durable traits and relationships.

Type-scenes make variation legible. Hospitality commonly moves through arrival, reception, food, questioning, gift, and departure; a host's delay, violence, or generosity gains force against that sequence. Recognition scenes likewise repeat a test of signs, but the scar, bow, bed, and orchard disclose different relationships.

## Translation as poetics

A translator decides whether repeated Greek expressions should repeat in English, whether metrical regularity outweighs local idiom, and whether performance energy should be visible on the page. These choices help explain why versions feel different while narrating the same action.
