## Four questions, not one

“The author of the *Odyssey*” can mean the traditional poet called Homer, the inherited singing tradition, the shaping of a monumental poem, or the textual transmission behind surviving manuscripts. The Homer Multitext project studies the epics as works shaped by oral tradition rather than as a single authored text; the [source record here](../external-sources/chs-homer-multitext.md) is metadata only and identifies the project rather than reproducing its argument, so the characterization is this wiki's own summary of a body of work it links to rather than a claim the record carries.

This does not make artistry accidental. It locates artistry within a tradition able to preserve formulas, scenes, and story patterns while recomposing them.

## Evidence inside the poem

Repeated epithets, speech patterns, dawn formulas, feasts, sacrifices, arrivals, baths, and recognitions appear throughout the complete text preserved here.[^butler] Repetition supplies compositional resources and expectations; variation against them carries meaning.

The poem is also architecturally ambitious. Telemachus's journey, Odysseus's first-person wanderings, the Ithacan return, and the recognitions are nested and delayed with control. Oral poetics and large-scale design are not opposites.

## Why it matters

A cautious account separates tradition, performance, monumental composition, and manuscript history. “Homer” remains a useful traditional name, but it cannot settle every historical question the evidence leaves open.
