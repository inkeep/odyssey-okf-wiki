## Two preserved texts and a pointer

The preserved PerseusDL XML provides a complete machine-readable Ancient Greek text.[^greek] The Butler edition provides a complete, searchable public-domain English prose translation.[^butler] The Homer Multitext record is a metadata-only pointer rather than a preserved text: it identifies the project and its editorial approach so that a reader can go to the source themselves, but the framework it describes is not reproduced here.[^chs]

None substitutes perfectly for the others. Greek is necessary when an argument depends on a repeated word, epithet, ambiguity, or line structure. Butler is useful for navigation and broad narrative comparison but suppresses verse form. Oral-traditional scholarship explains why repetition and variation deserve analysis rather than dismissal.

## Responsible comparison

- Cite book and line numbers whenever wording matters.
- Treat translations as interpretations, not transparent containers.
- Separate manuscript variation from translator variation.
- Do not infer social meaning from one English synonym without checking the Greek term and context.
- Compare recurrent scenes across the poem before declaring an episode exceptional.
- State when an observation is secure but its interpretation remains debated.

## High-value passages

The opening invocation, the Cyclops's violation of hospitality, the underworld encounter with Achilles, Eurycleia's recognition, the bow contest, the punishment of the enslaved women, Penelope's bed test, and Athena's imposed peace are especially productive points for comparing text, translation, and interpretation.
