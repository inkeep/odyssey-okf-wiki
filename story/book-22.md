## Why this book matters

The book is both judicial reckoning and terrifying massacre, forcing readers to examine how epic authorizes collective punishment.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The armed man standing in the hall is still taken for a beggar | He shoots Antinous through the throat mid-drink and names himself | Disguise ends; the suitors find the doors barred and the hall stripped of the arms that hung there |
| Eurymachus offers full restitution in bronze and gold | Odysseus refuses any payment short of their lives | The killing is framed as judgment rather than plunder — until Melanthius arms them from the store-room and is caught at it |
| The hall is full of dead and the household must be sorted | Eurycleia names twelve unfaithful maids; they carry out the bodies and are hanged | Justice and atrocity become indistinguishable in one act — and Odysseus silences Eurycleia's cry of triumph over the dead |
