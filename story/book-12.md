## Why this book matters

Knowledge cannot abolish loss, and hunger turns a known prohibition into collective catastrophe.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circe's sailing directions name dangers no crew could face freely | He has himself lashed to the mast and stops the crew's ears with wax | The Sirens' song is heard and survived — knowledge without surrender |
| Scylla and Charybdis offer no good choice, only a cheaper loss | On Circe's advice he steers toward Scylla and says nothing to the crew | Six men are taken alive; foreknowledge does not prevent the loss it predicts |
| Storm-bound on Thrinacia with the provisions gone | Eurylochus persuades the starving crew to kill Helios's cattle | Zeus destroys the ship; Odysseus alone survives, drifting past Charybdis to Ogygia |
