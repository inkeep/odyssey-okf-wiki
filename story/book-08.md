## Why this book matters

Performance pierces disguise: the hidden hero becomes legible through his involuntary response to art.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Phaeacians will convey a guest whose name they still do not know | Alcinous calls a feast and games in his honor | Demodocus sings of Odysseus and Achilles at Troy, and the guest weeps behind his cloak |
| Euryalus taunts him as a trader rather than an athlete | Odysseus throws the discus past every mark and challenges the hall | Standing is re-established by deed; Euryalus apologizes with the gift of a sword |
| Demodocus sings the wooden horse at Odysseus's own request | He weeps a third time, and this time Alcinous is watching | The king demands his name — the story must now be told in the first person |
