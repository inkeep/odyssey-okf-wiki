## Why this book matters

A humble hut practices the hospitality the palace has abandoned; false autobiography creates space for ethical testing.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The disguised king reaches his own farm and is set on by the dogs | Eumaeus drives them off and takes the beggar in without asking his name | The hut keeps the hospitality the palace has abandoned |
| The beggar must be plausible without being identifiable | He invents a Cretan life story and swears his master is close at hand | Eumaeus, burned by earlier liars, refuses to believe it — and feeds him anyway |
| A cold night tests whether a host will give up his own covering | Odysseus hints for a cloak by telling a story about one | Eumaeus lends the spare cloak he keeps for bad weather, saying plainly that no one there has two and that the guest takes his rags back in the morning |
