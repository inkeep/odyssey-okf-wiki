## Why this book matters

Comedy and sacrilege coexist on the final day, while repeated signs make ignorance culpable.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sleepless night, and the disloyal maids laugh their way out to the suitors | He rebukes his own heart and lies still, as he lay still in the Cyclops's cave | Athena promises success; Zeus answers with thunder, and a mill-woman curses the suitors aloud |
| The last feast begins and the suitors' violence turns open | Ctesippus picks a heifer's foot out of the meat-basket and throws it at the beggar for sport | Telemachus rebukes the hall as its master, and Agelaus answers for the suitors |
| The signs multiply and are taken for jokes | Theoclymenus sees the walls running with blood and the hall crowded with ghosts | He walks out while they laugh — ignorance becomes culpable |
