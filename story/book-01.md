> The gods debate Odysseus's detention. Athena, disguised as Mentes, urges Telemachus to confront the suitors and seek news.[^butler]

## Why this book matters

The poem begins away from its hero. The crisis is first political and domestic: a household without an acknowledged ruler.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Odysseus is held on Ogygia while Poseidon is absent from the gods' council | Athena puts his case to Zeus and wins a decision | Athena proposes sending Hermes to Calypso; the return is set in motion, though Hermes is not dispatched until Book 5 |
| The suitors devour a household with no acknowledged master | Athena arrives as Mentes and counsels Telemachus as a guest-friend | Telemachus is roused to seek news and claim his father's standing |
| Phemius's song of the bitter homecomings makes Penelope weep | Telemachus sends his mother to her rooms and speaks to the hall himself | He asserts household authority for the first time and calls an assembly |
