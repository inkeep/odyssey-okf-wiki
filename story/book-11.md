## Why this book matters

The poem's temporal center looks backward and forward at once, making homecoming answerable to the dead.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circe will not let him sail home until he consults a dead prophet | He digs the trench at the edge of Hades and holds the shades off with his sword | Elpenor comes first, unburied, and claims his rites before anyone else may speak |
| He needs to know how, and whether, the return ends | Tiresias tells him of Thrinacia, the suitors, and the oar carried inland | The homecoming acquires conditions — and a life that continues past the poem |
| Anticlea, Agamemnon, and Achilles each bring a different verdict on return | He hears of his mother's death, a king murdered at his own hearth, and a hero who would rather be a living serf | Return is redefined as fragile and worth more than glory — then the crowding dead unnerve him and he breaks off |
