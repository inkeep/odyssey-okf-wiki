## Why this book matters

The narrative catches up with itself: narrated wandering becomes present-tense homecoming, but arrival begins with misrecognition.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The tale is told, and the Phaeacians must make good on their promise | They load the gifts and row him home while he sleeps | He arrives in Ithaca unconscious, having crossed the sea without effort |
| Poseidon protests that mortals are ferrying his enemy home unharmed | Zeus lets him turn the returning ship to stone within sight of harbor | Scheria closes itself off; the age of effortless passage ends |
| He wakes in a mist and does not recognize his own island | Athena, in the shape of a young shepherd, tests him and he answers with a Cretan lie | She laughs, reveals herself and the island, and disguises him as a beggar |
