## Why this book matters

The ending extends nostos from private reunion to intergenerational and civic settlement, while divine force closes an otherwise renewable feud.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The suitors' shades arrive in Hades among the heroes of Troy | Amphimedon recounts the slaughter; Agamemnon sets Penelope against Clytemnestra | The poem passes judgment on its own ending from the far side of death |
| Laertes is still alive in the orchard, filthy and sunk in grief | Odysseus tests him with a false story before naming the trees his father once gave him | Recognition reaches the third generation, and the old man arms himself |
| The dead suitors' kin gather under Eupithes to take blood revenge | Laertes kills Eupithes with a spear cast, and Athena stops the fight with a shout | Divine force closes a feud that private justice could only have renewed |
