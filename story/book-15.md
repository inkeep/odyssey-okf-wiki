> Athena recalls Telemachus from Sparta. He avoids the suitors' ambush, entrusts the seer Theoclymenus to Piraeus rather than taking him home, and goes first to the hut of Eumaeus.[^butler]

## Why this book matters

Separate return plots converge, while omens make the suitors' doomed blindness increasingly visible.[^butler]

## Dramatic movement

| Pressure | Response | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Telemachus lingers at Sparta while his estate is eaten at home | Athena wakes him and warns of the ambush waiting in the straits | He takes his leave, declines a longer tour, and sails by the outer route |
| A hunted seer asks for passage, and nothing obliges Telemachus to give it | He takes Theoclymenus aboard and answers for him | The son practises the hospitality the suitors have destroyed |
| In the hut, master and swineherd sit together as strangers | Eumaeus tells his own story — a king's son sold into slavery by his nurse | Loyalty is given a history, and Telemachus arrives at the door |
