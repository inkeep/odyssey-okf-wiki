> [!IMPORTANT]
> An adaptation is an interpretation with a new medium, audience, and historical pressure—not merely a checklist of retained episodes.

- [Translations: choosing an Odyssey](./translations.md) — Every translation re-decides meter, pace, repetition, names, tone, and the ethical pressure of key scenes.
- [James Joyce's Ulysses](./ulysses-joyce.md) — Joyce relocates an Odyssean day to Dublin and converts heroic wandering into urban perception, language, appetite, and domestic return.
- [O Brother, Where Art Thou?](./o-brother.md) — The Coen brothers translate wandering into Depression-era Mississippi, using musical quest, comic misrecognition, and loose Homeric echoes.
- [Film and television](./film-and-television.md) — Screen Odysseys range from direct spectacle to films about the impossibility of adapting Homer.
- [Literary retellings](./literature.md) — Modern writers often shift consciousness toward figures whom the epic contains but does not follow for long.
- [Visual art and illustration](./visual-art.md) — Odyssean images isolate charged instants and make viewers supply the surrounding narrative.
- [Comics and speculative fiction](./comics-and-speculative-fiction.md) — Graphic and speculative retellings exploit episodic monsters, mutable bodies, and expansive world-building.

## Comparative lens

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ancient epic | Ithaca and household | Endurance, intelligence, force | Oral-derived hexameter narrative |
| Modern novel | City and interior life | Attention, survival, ordinary care | Style change and interior monologue |
| Film | Image, landscape, body | Performance and spectacle | Montage, flashback, sound |
| Comics / SF | New world-system | Adaptability under altered rules | Sequential art and redesigned myth |
