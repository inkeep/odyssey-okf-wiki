> This register complements the main constellation with named figures who have brief appearances, appear inside songs or memories, or belong to the underworld catalogues. Names are given in their standard Greek forms, which is often not what Butler prints: his translation uses the Roman equivalents (Minerva, Jove, Neptune, Mercury, Diana, Venus, Mars, Vulcan, Proserpine) and its own spellings (Euryclea, Teiresias, Eupeithes, Alcmena, Clytoneus). The per-book counts on the story pages are taken over Butler's spellings, and [how these counts are made](../scholarship/counting-method.md) lists the correspondences in full.

Dedicated pages cover the poem's principal dramatic figures and the named secondary figures indexed above.[^butler] Unnamed groups—such as sailors, servants, island peoples, and shades—are treated through collective pages or within the relevant book guide. Genealogical names mentioned only to identify another person remain discoverable in the complete source text rather than being misleadingly presented as developed characters.
