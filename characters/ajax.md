> Greek warrior whose quarrel with Odysseus continued beyond death.

## Role in the poem

In the underworld he refuses Odysseus's attempt at reconciliation over the arms of Achilles.[^butler]

Butler's translation gives the name to two different men, and this guide is the greater Ajax, son of Telamon. The Ajax wrecked on the rocks of Gyrae in Book 4 — drowned after boasting he had escaped the sea in the gods' despite — is the lesser Ajax, a separate figure with no guide here — Butler names neither father in that passage, and calls this guide's subject "Ajax son of Telamon" only in Book 11. Book 4's entry in the [count list](../story/book-04.md) is therefore that man, not this one.[^butler]

## Interpretive profile

His silence is an anti-recognition: some heroic conflicts resist eloquent repair.[^butler]
