## Why brief figures matter

A character may occupy only a line yet still locate a household, complete a genealogy, transmit information, model hospitality, witness a ritual, or connect the *Odyssey* to another heroic tradition.[^butler] Page length should therefore follow interpretive function rather than fame.

## Six recurring functions

| Function | What to notice | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Messenger or witness | How private action becomes public knowledge | [Lampetie](./lampetie.md), [Noemon](./noemon.md) |
| Social calibrator | Courtesy, insult, compensation, or civic judgment | [Echeneus](./echeneus.md), [Euryalus](./euryalus.md) |
| Household memory | Knowledge that authenticates identity and continuity | [Actoris](./actoris.md), [Ctimene](./ctimene.md) |
| Collective differentiation | Variation inside groups too easily treated as uniform | [Amphinomus](./amphinomus.md), [Leodes](./leodes.md), [Agelaus](./agelaus.md) |
| Embedded tradition | A compressed allusion to stories beyond this plot | [Ariadne](./ariadne.md), [Eriphyle](./eriphyle.md), [Tyro](./tyro.md) |
| Structural marker | Repetition or personification that organizes epic time and space | [Eos](./eos.md), [Hades](./hades.md) |

## A disciplined reading sequence

First establish the exact book and immediate action. Next ask who names the figure and why the name is needed. Then compare the figure with others performing the same role. Finally distinguish what the passage says from what a later mythographic tradition adds.

This procedure prevents two opposite errors: dismissing short appearances as trivia and importing an entire external myth into a passage that uses only one part of it.
