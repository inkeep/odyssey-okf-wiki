## Role in the poem

Lord of the realm of the dead. His house is the destination of Odysseus's necromantic journey and the permanent boundary against return.[^butler]

Butler's translation names Hades thirty times, and twenty-five of those are the place rather than the person — "the house of Hades", "down to Hades". Only three clearly address the god, and two more are undecidable. He is therefore left out of the per-book counts on the story pages, since a figure of one or two would imply a presence in the poem he does not have; [how these counts are made](../scholarship/counting-method.md) sets out the reasoning.

## Where to read

Books 10 and 11, where Butler names the god rather than the place. He carries no count on the book pages, for the reason given above.
